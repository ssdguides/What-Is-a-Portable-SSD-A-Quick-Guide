# What Is a Portable SSD? A Quick Guide

A portable SSD is a pocket-sized external drive that stores data on NAND flash
and connects over USB, usually USB-C. It's faster and more shock-tolerant than
a typical external hard drive, but costs more per terabyte and is not a backup
by itself.

## When it makes sense
- Moving large folders between machines
- Offloading RAW photos and video projects
- Local backups before a reinstall or migration
- Extra storage on laptops with no free internal slot

## Quick comparison
| | Portable SSD | External HDD | USB flash drive | Cloud |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Speed | Fast | Slower | Varies widely | Depends on internet |
| Cost per TB | Higher | Lowest | Low at small sizes | Subscription |
| Best for | Active projects, travel | Archives | Documents, quick handoffs | Sharing, off-site copy |

## Things to check before buying
- **USB tier:** match the drive to your port (5, 10, 20, 40 or 80 Gbps)
- **Cable:** USB-C is a connector, not a speed
- **Capacity:** 1TB is a comfortable default, and leave headroom
- **File system:** exFAT works on both Windows and macOS
- **Durability:** only drives with a published IP rating are tested for dust and water
- **Backup:** follow the 3-2-1 rule (3 copies, 2 media types, 1 off-site)
